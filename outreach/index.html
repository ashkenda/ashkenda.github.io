iNaturalist

I am a huge fan of the iNaturalist community science platform and make sure to support it through my own volunteer efforts. As a result, I have been the lead organizer of the iNaturalist City Nature Challenge in Lansing for two years (2025 & 2026) and recently became an iNaturalist ambassador. The City Nature Challenge is an annual global bioblitz aimed at documenting wildlife in urban areas. As a local organizer, I scheduled and attended guided, taxon-focused nature walks led by local experts and advertised them to the community. I personally reached out to local experts on birds, plants, and insects to recruit walk leads and sourced funding to have coffee and donuts for participants as well as a prize for whoever made the most observations. The walks that I organized significantly increased local participation in the bioblitz, helped build connections with community members and organizations, and generated thousands of new data points to be used by scientists across the globe. Perhaps most importantly, they were super fun and informative! I hope to continue organizing future City Nature Challenges and other bioblitz events in the future. You can see our 2025 City Nature Challenge project here or our 2026 project here. I am also an avid user of the iNaturalist app, with over 2,000 observations and 200 identifications (on my way to Curator status!).

MSU Bug House

When I’m not too busy with research, I occasionally volunteer at the MSU Bug House. The Bug House is a collection of both preserved and live specimens used to teach visitors about insects and arachnids. Organizations like MSU classes or clubs can book tours of the Bug House, but it is also open to the public regularly. As a result, I have learned how to talk about bugs to both children and adults with a wide range of comfort levels. I love volunteering at the Bug House because even visitors with reservations about bugs tend to be curious and open-minded, and I can often see them become more comfortable with the specimens in real time (sometimes even enough to hold them!). I received an MSU Bug House fellowship for my volunteer efforts in 2025.

Vernal Pool Patrol

I love vernal pools! In spring of 2025, I became a volunteer for the Michigan Vernal Pool Patrol, a statewide monitoring program created to document locations and health of vernal pools across Michigan. Volunteers help verify the presence of vernal pools, monitor their status annually, and collect data on physical and biological characteristics of the pools. This data is collected using GIS and is used by the Michigan Natural Features Inventory, Michigan State Extension, and Michigan Vernal Pools Partnership to provide information to policymakers and landmanagers and inform management and conservaiton of vernal pools in Michigan. I love taking a closer look at vernal pools in the area and get especially excited when I get to see some of my favorite vernal pool inhabitants, spotted salamanders and fairy shrimp!
