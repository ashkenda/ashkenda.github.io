My master's research leverages national scale public datasets and machine learning to predict risk of the cyanotoxin microcystin across lakes in the U.S. Microcystin is produced by several taxa of cyanobacteria and has severe health consequences for plants and animals, including humans. The drivers of microcystin production are not entirely understood and span across chemical, biological, hydrological, landscape, and climate categories. I am using publically available data from the EPA, LAGOS, and CMIP6 as well as a gradient boosting machine learning model to understand which predictors of microcystin are most important, why some lakes are prone to high toxin concentrations in the present, and which lakes will be at greatest risk of microcystin in the future under different climate and land use scenarios. So far, I have presented this work at the International Conference on Harmful Algae (2025) and the Association for the Sciences of Limnology and Oceanography (2026). I plan to defend this work in either December of 2026 or the following spring. Publications to come soon!

In addition to my thesis work, I am also helping out with some additional projects in the Bridge Lab. Our undergraduate student, Abigail Lippert, is authoring a paper about how lake access impacts lake monitoring and how both access and monitoring differ in lakes in communities with varying percentages of people of color. I am contributing to this paper as a co-author and have helped with running some of the random forest analyses, designing figures, and writing. I am also a co-author on the associated data module that came out of this project, LAGOS-US HUMAN. The data module includes information about human communities surrounding lakes (based on the US census) as well as lake access points.

We are also working on an update to the LAGOS-US LIMNO data module. The update will incorporate more recent data from the Water Quality Portal, including information from lake sampling programs that have not made their data public until now. I contributed to this project by developing a harmonization and QA/QC protocol for measurements of microcystin toxins based on accepted methodologies. I also QA/QC'd data for some less commonly detected contaminants.
