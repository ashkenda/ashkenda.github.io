The University of Michigan Biological Station is a place that inspires me, both scientifically and creatively. When I was working there as a field technician in 2024, the sounds of waves crashing on the shore of the lake would sometimes remind me of one of my favorite films, The Lighthouse (2019, Robert Eggers). I pitched to my friends the possibility of making a tribute to the film and by the next day, we had a (rough) script adapted from the original. It would be a surreal Biostation-themed parody in which "the lighthouse" was the belltower that the kitchen staff at the station would ring to announce meals. I pushed my novice (i.e. nonexistent) directing skills to their limits while co-starring and editing our footage. When we finally had it ready, we screened _The Belltower_ to an audience on the last day of classes at the station. Despite only having filmed in our free time over two weeks, some of my friends delivered pretty stellar performances. 

A year later, I went with some friends to visit the Biostation again for a few days. My costar from the first film had been watching a lot of X-files and I was really into Werner Herzog at the time, so we decided this trip would be a perfect opportunity to make another film. This time, it was a mystery/thriller that drew heavy inspiration from the X-files and Herzog's found footage "Incident at Loch Ness" as well as the cryptic Douglas Lake Monster that we had heard tales of at the station. For our second film, we had no script other than a loose outline of our plot, and we recruited almost everyone we came across as actors. _The Dougie Files_ ended up having a much broader appeal (and possibly more cultural relevance) than our first endeavor and was incredibly fun to make. We even drew on a couple of ecological concepts for parts of our story.

Due to their PG-13 ratings, I won't make my films too easy to find on this site. If you're interested in watching them, you can find them on my Youtube channel, @KendallAsh-e2b.
