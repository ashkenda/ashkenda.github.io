One fateful day in the summer of 2025, I decided to take a break from coding by snooping around the lab to see what was hiding in our seldom-opened drawers and cabinets. Among old t-shirts and miscellaneous cords, I struck gold-- literally (well, kind of). I found an unlabeled bag full of plastic gold, silver, and bronze medals. No one in the lab had a clue why or by whom the medals had been purchased, and it was clear that they had no intended purpose in our lab other than collecting dust in a drawer. At the time, our group was known as the DILL Lab (for Data Intensive Landscape Limnology), and we had recently learned that two members of the lab would soon be moving on to other opportunities. This finding could mean only one thing: we had to have the DILLLympics as a final send-off.

Over the next couple of months, I budgeted some time to personalize our plastic medals and polled the lab to decide on our DILLLympics events. We decided on:
1) an iNaturalist race to get the most observations in a given time period
2) a worst figure competition (in good fun)
3) ping-pong, and 
4) a "DILL" themed snack contest.

On November 16th, a day that will go down in the history books, we met at Kendra's house for the competitions. Tough battles were fought intermittently between snacking and socialization. We documented the species around the property, witnessed Kendra's fierce ping-pong skills, had some laughs at a few very creatively designed figures, and enjoyed many dill flavored foods. We also watched Kendra's family turtle ride a skateboard around the house (very impressive) and made custom DILL Lab shirts using a couple of linocut designs I made. Overall, it was a great time and a fitting bon voyage for our departing colleagues.
