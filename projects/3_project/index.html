undergraduate research

working in the Duffy Lab at the University of Michigan

My senior year of undergrad (2023-2024) at the University of Michigan was spent working as a research assistant in the Duffy Lab. This experience was a combination of field work in the summer and lots of lab work and maintenance throughout the academic year. Over the summer, I learned how to take zooplankton tows and sonde measurements, and I had my first encounters with a secchi disk. Most of my time in the lab was spent feeding daphnia and parasite colonies, maintaining algal food reserves, filtering lake water, counting parasite spores, and prepping experiments for others in the lab (not to mention washing dishes of course). I loved learning about the science that others were working on in the lab, and many of my long-term scientific interests originated from projects that I assisted with during this time.

After my first summer in the lab, I had the priviledge of running my own experiment as a supplemental analysis for chapter 3 of Dr. Kristel Sanchez’s PhD dissertation (found here). Dr. Sanchez had used ddPCR to quantify the amount of algae eaten by Daphnia during a 24-hour experiment and found no notable difference between the amount of algal DNA in beakers before and after feeding. We wanted to determine whether this result was reliable or a consequence of persistent algal eDNA which had not been digested during feeding. To test this, I conducted a feeding assay with Daphnia magna over 24 hours and compared the estimated amount of algae with and without feeding using ddPCR with and without the addition of a light-activated dye that removes eDNA. For comparison, I also quantified the amount of algae with and without feeding using cell counting and fluorometry. Interestingly, while the dye effectively removed eDNA, I still found no statistical difference between dye and no-dye treatments in which feeding had occurred. This finding supported Dr. Sanchez’s results and suggested that eDNA was not responsible for the small observed differences in algal quantity before and after feeding.
